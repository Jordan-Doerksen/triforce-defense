In March of the Scourge, Arthas holds Hearthglen with towers, footmen and whatever the town can spare until Uther arrives. Triforce-Defense is that night, held by three. We are captains, each charged with one town of a border province. The province next door has already fallen to the horde. The King's army is coming. Until it does, the towns are ours to keep — or we go north in daylight and tear down the rituals summoning what comes at night.

The province is a graph before it is terrain. Three towns, joined by chokepoints. The hill town sits on high ground with a river through it and a waterfall on its eastern wall; the road down beside the falls is the only way to the valley. The valley town holds a lake inside its walls, and thin woods climb east out of it into the forest town, which the thick wood hides. The hill and the forest do not touch — except through the north, where the ground is open, blighted, and theirs. The safe road is slow. The quick road runs through the enemy. Every time a captain calls for help, that is the decision.

Less macro, on purpose. The garrison does the chores. Resource sites are placed and never run dry. Buildings go on plots, and there are fewer plots than things to build. What remains for a captain is the part worth doing: the gate, the field company, the hero, and the question every night asks — hold, or move.

Built in Warcraft III because everyone at the table owns it, will play it, and the editor supplies the terrain, the data and Lua. The ceiling is a map the group plays. Said aloud, so it stays a ceiling and never becomes a disappointment.
