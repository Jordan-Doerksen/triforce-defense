Hearthglen, but three of us.

Three captains, three towns, one province — and the one next door has already fallen.

In March of the Scourge, Arthas holds Hearthglen with towers, footmen and whatever the town can spare until Uther arrives. Triforce-Defense is that night, held by three. We are captains, each charged with one town of a border province. The province next door has already fallen to the horde. The King's army is coming. Until it does, the towns are ours to keep — or we go north in daylight and tear down the rituals summoning what comes at night.

The province is a graph before it is terrain. Three towns, joined by chokepoints. Mountains wall the province on the west and thick forest on the east. The hill town sits on high ground with a river through it and a waterfall on its eastern wall; the road down beside the falls, over the bridge, is the safe road to the valley. The valley town holds a lake inside its walls, and thin woods climb east out of it into the forest town, which the thick wood hides. The hill and the forest do not touch — except through the north, where the ground is open, blighted, and theirs. The safe road is slow. The quick road runs through the enemy. Every time a captain calls for help, that is the decision.

Less macro, on purpose. The garrison does the chores. Resource sites are placed and never run dry. Buildings go on plots, and there are fewer plots than things to build. What remains for a captain is the part worth doing: the gate, the field company, the hero, and the question every night asks — hold, or move.

Built in Warcraft III because everyone at the table owns it, will play it, and the editor supplies the terrain, the data and Lua. The ceiling is a map the group plays. Said aloud, so it stays a ceiling and never becomes a disappointment.

Go north in daylight.

Twelve nights until the King's army. Each dusk is a deadline.

Approvedthe wave
comes at night
Approvedweak by day,
capped on blight
Approveda broken ritual
takes its blight

The clock is the sun. The dead come at night, down the short roads, to the gates. By day they are weak, on open ground and on their own blight, and the north is open to whoever goes. That is the whole rule. The map is meant to teach it without a word of text.

Strength has two settings and a cap. Daylight takes something off the dead; blight gives some of it back. At night the dead are at full strength, and stronger on blight. By day the cap keeps them below full strength everywhere, the rituals included, which is where the push is going. A mark on each unit shows the day; the ground shows the blight.

Dusk is announced. The sky turns, a horn sounds, each gate is marked on the minimap, and the dead recover over a short ramp, not all at once. The camera stays where the captain left it. A company still in the north at dusk was told.

A match starts at dawn, with a longer first day to build. It runs twelve nights by default, and one line at the edge of the screen says which. The King's army arrives at the dawn after the last night. The rituals are the only undead holdings in the north. Each one broken in daylight takes its blight with it and weakens what comes at the end.

Time, never safety.

Someone stays at the gate. The captain decides who else does.

is not yours
Approvedno road

Approveda talent for

Each town has two armies. The garrison belongs to the town. It walks out of the Barracks on its own, stands at the gate, and takes no orders from anyone. The Barracks decides how many and what kind: footmen first, then ranged units, then casters. A Blacksmith decides how good they are. A post lost at night comes back slowly, about two thirds of the way by the next dusk, so a hard night is still there the evening after.

The field force belongs to the captain. It stands at its own gate until he moves it. When he does, the gate is the garrison's alone, and the garrison buys time with its men. It never buys safety. When the garrison is nearly gone, a horn sounds and the gate shows on the minimap, wherever the captain happens to be.

Each captain leads with a commander. The Engineer breaks things, with the Siege Engines. The Knight-Marshal moves, with the Knights. The Castellan holds, with the Templars. Their abilities are stock Warcraft III abilities with the numbers changed. None of them moves an army anywhere except along a road, and no ability raises the dead. A commander who falls returns at his town after a delay.

Commanders carry gear in the new equipment slots, and the loot of the wild is gear. Every second dawn a captain's gate is still standing, he earns a talent: six in a full match, if the gate holds.
