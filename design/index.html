Hearthglen, but three of us.

Chapter 1Decisions 1.1 · 1.2Sealed 15 Oct 2026

In March of the Scourge, Arthas holds Hearthglen with towers, footmen and whatever the town can spare until Uther arrives. Triforce-Defense is that night, held by three. We are captains, each charged with one town of a border province. The province next door has already fallen to the horde. The King's army is coming. Until it does, the towns are ours to keep, or we go north in daylight and tear down the rituals summoning what comes at night.

Why Warcraft III

Everyone at the table owns it, will play it, and the editor supplies the terrain, the data and Lua. It solves the three things that killed the last games: no players, no level design, engine work.

The ceiling is a map the group plays. Said aloud, so it stays a ceiling and never becomes a disappointment.

Less macro, on purpose

The garrison does the chores. Resource sites are placed and never run dry. Buildings go on plots, and there are fewer plots than things to build. What remains for a captain is the part worth doing: the gate, the field company, the hero, and the question every night asks.

The fire. Before the first day.

Chapter 4Decisions 4.1 to 4.4Sealed 17 Oct 2026

The match opens in the dark, around a fire. A love letter to Diablo II's character screen: as each captain takes a charge, their champion walks into the firelight and stands with the others. Embers rise, the fire flickers on the figures, a hound sleeps, crows sit a dead tree, the towns' standards hang, thunder somewhere far. The default UI is gone; the Lua frames float at the edges. This is the one place the no-hijack law does not bind, so the set piece goes here.

The three charges

Each captain takes a town with one button: first come first served, no swapping, no override. The charge shows the town's T3 and its lean, so the pick is a role pick. On the click your champion walks to the fire.

The host's panel

Two settings at version one: the mode, 13 nights or the hard mode's 26, and one difficulty dial that scales wave size and undead stats. Nothing else ships first.

Every choice is synced to every client before the first day, or the game desyncs.

The fallback

Stock dialogs, wired behind one flag in config. They must always start a legal match with the same settings. The war council is the first Lua deliverable, prototyped in Phase 1 with the mode setting synced.

The first day's work: plots, sites, the forge.

Chapters 2 · 6Decisions 2.2 · 2.5 · 2.6 · 6.1 to 6.6

Plots

Buildings go on authored plots only. A plot is an owned Circle of Power; its command card lists what it can become. Choose, and the plot builds itself in view, then is that building. Six plots, nine options: Town Hall opens two, Keep two, Castle the last two. The Barracks takes one, so five real choices. Same count and menu in every town.

The nine, sealed. The sites (mill, mine) are not plots; they stay pre-placed. No plot menu may contain a strictly-best option: that is one fewer plot, not a fork. Demolish-to-circle is absent or costly.

Offensive

Trains units the player controls. The move spend.

Defensive

Towers, and buildings that spawn uncontrolled defenders. The hold spend.

Resource

The sites. Never deplete; a finite upgrade ladder. The pay spend.

The keystone

The Barracks does both: its level feeds the garrison, its queue trains the player's footmen. Named, not categorised. A second hybrid building makes Hybrid a real category; until then, no.

The site ladder

Trees stand, the mine never runs out. Each mill and mine starts with one peasant and takes up to five; each next peasant costs more, and every one you buy walks in and works the site. One town lumber-rich, one gold-rich, one balanced; no town out-earns another in total.

Main-hall tiers

Town Hall: the first ring of two plots. Keep: the second ring, blacksmith tiers 3 and 4. Castle: the last two plots, the T3, tiers 5 and 6, the hero's final ability tier. Every tier also raises the garrison. The town's growth is visible on the ground as rings light up.

Set pieces

Every plot builds itself in view: scaffolds, peasants walking in, doodads filling. No camera move, ever. Built, it stays alive, and a click opens a small window with rate or stock and the next upgrade.

The market

Potions, home portals, a boot and a ring at Town Hall; the next step of each at Keep; one rare at Castle, rolled at setup. The good items only come from the wild.

Dusk: hold, or move.

Chores to the garrison, the interesting part to the player. Every wave the spend is one question: hold, or move. You cannot fund both, and the field army leaving should visibly thin the gate.

Autonomous. The Barracks produces soldiers on its own that rally to the gate. Barracks level sets rate and quality.

The nights: thirteen, and four of them marked.

Chapters 1 · 7Decisions 1.4 · 7.1Pick a mode

Day and night are the clock. A match is thirteen nights; a hard mode, planned for later, doubles it. The undead take a large debuff by day and a large buff on blight, and the rituals and undead bases sit on blight. The map teaches the push window with no rule text: you push in daylight.

Event nights · 3, 6, 9, 12

Known in advance. The event is drawn from a pool and announced at dusk with a visible tell: an undead air raid, cultist insurgents raising the dead inside a base, an evil fog that cuts vision, and more later. In hard mode, every third night. An event never wipes a town by itself: the garrison must still be able to buy time.

The pool's mechanics are open. Day length and buff values live in config and are open; the night buff needs a visible tell on the unit.

The wild: the overrun province.

Chapters 1 · 3Decisions 1.3 · 3.1 · 3.2Click a ritual

The north has three reasons to exist: the shortcut, the rituals, and the loot. Both endings live in the same region the players already have reasons to enter.

Win A · hold

Survive to the final wave and hold until relief. The King's army arrives.

Win B · break

Destroy the three cultist rituals in the north: an obelisk with acolytes, or similar. Each ritual destroyed weakens the final boss. All three destroyed ends the match early. Partial credit means a one-for-three team feels the difference.

The final boss · full strength

Loot

Custom items, new or juiced edits of stock, earned by side objectives in the wild and by killing undead champions. Drops are team-fair: a shared stash or a drop per player, never to whoever clicked. Loot is what the group talks about afterwards.

Vignettes · flavour

Clicking a resource site between waves may lock the camera to an authored shot with a Lua frame showing rate and the next upgrade. Dressing, approved as dressing. Skippable, and it never fires during a wave.

Re-run the council if the rituals move out of the north or stop feeding the boss. What each ritual takes from the boss, and acolyte behaviour, are open.

Non-negotiable.

ConstraintsEvery agent reads these first
  • Lua first. Scripting in Lua; the trigger editor for what GUI does better; JASS only where Lua cannot reach.
  • Authored terrain. Every resource site, build plot, gate and tower slot is placed by the map author. The player decides what, never where.
  • Never hijack a camera mid-wave. Vignettes are between-wave dressing. A co-op partner losing camera control while a wall breaches uninstalls the map.
  • The garrison buys time, never safety. It holds normal waves at its level and never holds a spike or a boss alone.
  • Design before data editor. No unit, building or trigger is built before its decision is sealed in the manifest.
  • The bridge. The hill to valley road crosses the river at a bridge. The owner loves bridges.
  • Stock models only. Nothing is downloaded or installed. Anything already in the game's files is fair, and the campaign models are a must: all the variety the game has, changed only by data.
  • A setup phase. The match opens with a calm, planned setup for the host, built with Lua frames: modes, modifiers, settings, a full presentation layer. The game is simple in essence; the presentation goes all out.
