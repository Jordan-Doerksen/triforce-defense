In March of the Scourge, Arthas holds Hearthglen with towers, footmen and whatever the town can spare until Uther arrives. Triforce-Defense is that night as a three-player map. Each of us holds a walled province; the undead own the wild to the north; the match ends when the last wave is held — or when we go north in daylight and tear down the rituals summoning it.

The realm is a graph before it is terrain. Three provinces in the south, joined by chokepoints — a bridge over the river, a road through the wood. The north is open ground, dangerous, undead-held, and it is the fast way between any two provinces. The safe road is slow. The quick road runs through the enemy. Every time a friend calls for help, that is the decision.

Less macro, on purpose. The garrison does the chores. Resource sites are placed and never run dry. Buildings go on plots, and there are fewer plots than things to build. What remains for the player is the part worth doing: the gate, the field army, the hero, and the question every night asks — hold, or move.

Built in Warcraft III because everyone at the table owns it, will play it, and the editor supplies the terrain, the data and Lua. The ceiling is a map the group plays. Said aloud, so it stays a ceiling and never becomes a disappointment.
