In March of the Scourge, Arthas holds Hearthglen with towers, footmen and whatever the town can spare until Uther arrives. Triforce-Defense is that mission as a three-player co-op map: each of us holds a walled province, the undead own the wild to the north, and the match ends when the final wave is held — or when we go north in daylight and tear down the rituals that are summoning it.

The map is a graph before it is terrain. Three provinces in the south, joined by chokepoints — a bridge over a river, a path through the woods. The north is open ground, dangerous, undead-held, and it is the fast way between any two provinces. The safe road is slow. The quick road runs through the enemy. Every time a friend calls for help, that is the decision.

Less macro, on purpose. The garrison does the chores. Resource sites are placed and never run dry. Buildings go on plots, and there are fewer plots than things to build. What is left for the player is the part worth doing: the gate, the field army, the hero, and the question every night asks — hold, or move.

Built in Warcraft III Reforged because everyone in the group owns it, will play it, and the editor supplies the terrain tools, the data editor and Lua. The ceiling is a map the group plays. Said out loud so it stays a ceiling and never becomes a disappointment.
